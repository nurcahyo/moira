# Contributor Licence Agreement

**Required before your first contribution is merged.** It is short, it is a one-time
signature, and it covers everything you contribute afterwards.

If you are only reporting a bug, opening an issue, or reviewing someone else's work, this
does not apply to you.

---

## Why this exists

Moira is Apache-2.0 and will stay usable by anyone under those terms. But Apache-2.0 alone
does not give the project the right to **relicense** — including offering the same code under
a commercial licence to someone whose employer forbids Apache-2.0 attribution requirements, or
adopting a different open-source licence later if that becomes necessary.

Without a CLA, every contributor's copyright is separately held. Changing the licence would
then need each contributor's individual agreement, and in a project of any age some are
unreachable. Projects that skipped this step have been unable to fix their own licensing
years later. Collecting it at the first contribution is the cheapest moment it will ever be.

**This is not a copyright assignment. You keep your copyright.** You grant a licence
alongside it.

## How to sign

Comment on your first pull request with:

```
I have read the CLA and I agree to it, on behalf of myself
and, where applicable, my employer.

Signed: <your full name> <your email>
```

Once recorded, it covers all your future contributions. You will not be asked again.

Contributing on behalf of a company? Say so in the signature, and make sure whoever signs is
authorised to bind it.

## The honest note

A CLA asks something real of you. It lets one party relicense work you wrote, and reasonable
people decline on principle. If that is your position, **say so** — the alternative is that
your contribution is declined silently, which serves neither of us. Some changes can be
described in an issue and implemented independently, and that path stays open.

The intent is not to take your work private. Moira is Apache-2.0 and every published version
stays Apache-2.0 permanently — a licence, once granted, cannot be withdrawn from anyone who
already has it.
